# Citizen's Voice: 5-minute demo video script

For recording the submission video. **VO** = voice-over (what you say). **SCREEN** = what is on screen.
About 700 spoken words: speak calmly, don't rush. Record each scene separately and join them;
re-record a scene, not the whole video.

The two heroes are **Sarvam** (the ears and the mouth) and **Gemma 4** (the brain and the eyes).
Say their names every time they act.

---

## Scene 1: The problem (0:00–0:35)

**SCREEN:** a dark street at night (stock photo or your own), then a basic keypad phone.

**VO:**
> Meet Lakshmamma. She is 62 and lives in Dasarahalli. The streetlight outside her house has been
> broken for two weeks, and the lane is dark when she walks home.
> To complain today she needs a smartphone app, she needs to write in English, and she needs an
> exact address. She has none of these. And people who do complain usually never hear back.
> Complaints get lost, in the wrong language, in the wrong place, with no answer.

## Scene 2: Our answer, and the two heroes (0:35–1:00)

**SCREEN:** the title slide, then the "Meet the clerk" slide (Sarvam + Gemma).

**VO:**
> Citizen's Voice is a government complaints clerk that anyone can talk to: a phone call, a
> Telegram message, or a web page. No app, no English, no address needed.
> Two AI models do the clerk's job. **Sarvam** is the ears and the mouth: it understands Kannada,
> Hindi and English, even mixed in one sentence, and speaks back in a natural voice.
> **Gemma 4** is the brain and the eyes: it asks the right questions, finds the exact place, picks
> the right office, and looks at photos. Two minutes on the phone, and the complaint is filed.

## Scene 3: The phone call (1:00–1:50)

**SCREEN:** split screen: your phone on speaker on the left, the officer's Live desk on the right.

**ACTION:** Call the helpline. Speak Kannada, e.g. *"ನಮ್ಮ ಬೀದಿಯಲ್ಲಿ ಎರಡು ವಾರದಿಂದ ಲೈಟ್ ಇಲ್ಲ"* (no light on our
street for two weeks). Name a place. Let the clerk read it back, say yes, hear the tracking number.

**VO (over the call, short lines between turns):**
> Sarvam turns her Kannada into text. Gemma 4 decides what to ask next: where exactly?
> Gemma doesn't guess coordinates. It looks the place up in real map data, all of Bengaluru, and
> reads it back. Nothing is filed until she says yes.
> And here it is on the officer's desk, live, with a pin on the map. She gets a tracking number she
> can say back to us any time.

**ACTION (second, short call):** call again and ask *"what happened to my complaint?"*

**VO:**
> When she calls again, Gemma tells her the office, the deadline, and the latest update, in her
> language.

## Scene 4: Telegram (1:50–2:40)

**SCREEN:** Telegram on a phone (screen-recorded), dashboard in a corner.

**ACTION, in this order:**
1. **Text:** "pothole on MG Road". The clerk asks *which MG Road*, naming the areas. Pick one.
2. **Voice note:** hold the mic, speak a few words in Kannada or Hindi. Show the reply.
3. **Photo:** send a photo of a pothole or garbage.
4. **Location:** send a location pin or a Google Maps link.

**VO:**
> On Telegram you can type, speak, or send a photo.
> "MG Road": Bengaluru has more than one. Gemma asks which one, by area, instead of guessing.
> A voice note goes through Sarvam, just like the phone.
> A photo goes to Gemma's eyes: it sees what the problem is and how serious, so it doesn't ask
> what the photo already shows. A location pin or a Maps link gives the exact spot.

**ACTION (later in the video, cut it in here):** show the Telegram updates arriving: "work started"
with the officer's note **in Kannada**, followed by "(Officer wrote: …)".

**VO:**
> Every time the officer acts, she gets a message. The officer writes in English; Sarvam translates
> the note into her language.

## Scene 5: The web page (2:40–3:05)

**SCREEN:** the `/try` page in a browser.

**ACTION:** press the mic and speak, or type *"road alli tumba potholes ide"*. Point at the
**Gemma's steps** panel as it fills. After filing, enter a mobile number.

**VO:**
> No phone line? The same clerk works in any browser, by voice or by typing.
> On the right you can watch Gemma think: what it looked up, which place it chose, why it picked
> that office. Leave a mobile number, and we call you back when it's fixed.

## Scene 6: The officer's side (3:05–4:00)

**SCREEN:** the Live desk.

**VO, map first:**
> This is the officer's Live desk. Every complaint is a pin, placed from map data, never by the AI.
> The colour is urgency: grey and teal are minor, amber and orange are serious, red is an emergency.
> The number on a pin is how many people reported the same problem. When a second person reports
> the same pothole nearby, Gemma adds them to the first complaint instead of creating a duplicate.
> The count goes up and so does its priority.

**ACTION:** open a complaint. Show the conversation, then **Gemma's steps**.

**VO:**
> Gemma's steps show every decision it made, so an officer can check it.

**ACTION:** officer signs in → **Start work** with a note → **Add photo** (the fix photo) → **Mark fixed**.
The **Photo check** card appears.

**VO:**
> When the officer marks it fixed, Gemma 4 compares the fix photo with the citizen's photo: is it
> the same place, what matches, what changed. That's advice, not a verdict.
> Because the citizen decides.

**ACTION:** the phone rings (the call-back). Answer *"ಇಲ್ಲ, ಇನ್ನೂ ಕತ್ತಲು ಇದೆ"* (no, still dark).
Refresh: the complaint is **reopened**.

**VO:**
> Our system calls her back and asks: is it really fixed? She says no, and the complaint reopens
> with higher priority. The officer said fixed. The citizen said no. The citizen wins.

## Scene 7: Emergencies (4:00–4:20)

**SCREEN:** a call or Telegram message: *"a power line fell on the road and the water is touching
it"* (danger) or *"the water pipe burst, water is gushing out"* (resource loss).

**VO:**
> Some problems can't wait. If someone's life is in danger, a fallen live wire or a gas leak, Gemma
> marks it an emergency and tells the caller to dial 112. If a resource is being wasted by the
> minute, like a burst water main, that's an emergency too. Emergencies jump to the top, and every
> officer's dashboard gets an alert straight away.

> Record this scene only if the "danger / resource loss" alert is live when you record. If not,
> show the existing emergency flag and say only the first two sentences.

## Scene 8: Morning brief and the public scorecard (4:20–4:40)

**SCREEN:** the Morning brief page, click a card; then **Offices**.

**VO:**
> Every morning, officers get a brief: what's new, what's overdue, which office is behind, where
> problems are clustering. Every number is counted by code. Gemma only writes the sentences, and
> we check that it never invents a number. Click a card to go straight to those complaints.
> And the scorecard shows each office's record in public: fixed, open, overdue.

## Scene 9: Open source, and close (4:40–5:00)

**SCREEN:** the architecture slide, then the GitHub repo page.

**VO:**
> Gemma 4 is an open-weights model. Places come from OpenStreetMap. The app is FastAPI, React,
> Leaflet and Postgres, and our whole project is open source under Apache 2.0.
> Any phone, three languages, no app. Sarvam listens and speaks. Gemma thinks and sees.
> And the citizen always gets the last word. This is Citizen's Voice.

## Before you record

| Check | Why |
|---|---|
| Record **between 9 AM and 8 PM IST** | The call-back only rings in those hours |
| Run the reset and smoke test first (see handoff) | A clean desk, no test complaints |
| Prepare one Telegram complaint **with a photo** before Scene 6 | So the Photo check shows both photos side by side |
| Have a fix photo on the laptop | For "Mark fixed" |
| Phone ringer loud, Do Not Disturb off | The call-back comes from our number, not a saved contact |
| Officer passcode ready, signed in on the Live desk | Saves time on screen |
| Hide notifications, close other tabs | Clean recording |

**If the call-back doesn't ring within 20 s:** cut the waiting, then show the reopened complaint
from a call-back you recorded earlier, or say "the call-back is on its way" and move on.
**If Gemma is slow** (Google's API sometimes is): cut the silence in editing. Don't fake answers.
